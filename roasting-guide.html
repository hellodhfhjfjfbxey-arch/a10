Roasting Basics

Roasting is simple: dry heat, a hot oven and patience. Get five things right and almost any joint or bird comes out well.

  • Oven180–220°C
  • Check withA probe
  • Rest10–30 min
01

Bring it up and dry it off

Take meat out of the fridge 30 to 60 minutes before roasting so it cooks more evenly. Pat the surface dry with kitchen paper: a dry surface browns, a wet one steams. Season generously with salt, ideally an hour or more ahead.

02

Get the heat right

Preheat the oven fully. Many cooks start hot to brown the outside, then lower the heat to cook the middle gently.

  • Small, tender cuts and birds: 200 to 220°C throughout.
  • Large joints: start at 220°C for 20 minutes, then 160 to 180°C.
  • Fan ovens: reduce by about 20°C.
03

Use a rack and the right tin

A rack or a bed of thickly sliced onions lifts the meat so hot air reaches underneath. Choose a tin only a little bigger than the joint: too large and the juices burn, too small and it steams.

04

Check with a thermometer

Timings are only a guide, because ovens and joints differ. Push a probe thermometer into the thickest part, away from bone, and take the meat out a few degrees before your target. Poultry is done at 74°C in the thickest part of the thigh.

05

Rest before carving

Cover loosely with foil and rest for 10 minutes for a chicken and up to 30 minutes for a large joint. The juices settle back into the meat and the temperature evens out. Use this time to make gravy from the tin juices.
